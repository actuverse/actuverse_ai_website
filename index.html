AI, Defined

What is AI?

AI with insurance models,
not language models

At Actuverse, we are developing neural networks specifically for insurance claims modelling. These machine learning models are designed to learn from insurance data and estimate claim outcomes and their associated uncertainty. These are not language models repackaged for actuarial use.

Our ambition is to build a common modelling foundation across reserving, reserve risk, capital modelling and pricing. The focus is the insurance risk itself: how claims develop, what they may ultimately cost, and how uncertain those outcomes are.

The wider AI landscape

"AI" is a term that covers a broad range of technologies, often used interchangeably with Machine Learning and Deep Learning, and it helps to be specific about which ones we mean, especially as all these areas are developing fast. The categories below aren't strictly separate: generative AI and agentic tools are themselves built on deep learning. The application and architecture are what make a fundamental difference, and it's that difference in use that matters most here.

Generative AI, LLMs and chatbots

Tools like ChatGPT and Claude that generate text and images, answer questions, and hold conversations. Increasingly used for drafting, summarising, and research support.

Agentic AI

Systems that take actions on your behalf: booking things, navigating software, completing multi-step tasks with limited human input. Agentic AI is what you get when a chat LLM is harnessed with tools and instructions, and left to act on them.

Machine learning and deep learning

Statistical models trained on data to find patterns, make predictions, or estimate outcomes. This includes techniques like gradient boosting and neural networks, used for tasks such as predicting claims development, pricing risk, capital modelling, and reserve risk.
